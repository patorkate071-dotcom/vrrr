Gallery 01 · Effects

Effects of Video Games

Video games are often portrayed negatively, especially when society connects violent games to violent behavior. However, the module presents a more balanced perspective.

More Than Just a Game. The American Psychological Association states that there is no causal relationship between violent video games and violent behavior. At the same time, research identifies several possible cognitive and affective benefits of playing video games.

🧠 Cognitive Benefits Spatial navigation, reasoning, memory, perception, problem-solving, multitasking, response time, and visual skills.
🌼 Affective Benefits Improve mood, promote relaxation, and help ward off anxiety.
⚠️
Concerns still matter: addiction, depression, and aggression are issues worth discussing. The module calls for a balanced perspective rather than a one-sided view.

Gallery 02 · Failure

Learning Through Failure

In games, failure usually does not mean the end. It means learning, adjusting, and trying again.

Game Over Doesn't Mean Give Up. One benefit discussed in the module is the way video games can teach players to appreciate the value of failure. In a game, failing usually means trying again. Players learn to persist until they eventually succeed. Video games can therefore become environments where players practice problem-solving and persistence.

💬 Q&A: What can failure teach us?

Failure can teach resilience, adaptation, problem-solving, and patience. In games, every unsuccessful attempt can become information for the next attempt.

Gallery 03 · Gamification

Gamification

What if learning worked more like a game? Gamification brings game mechanics into activities that are not necessarily games.

What If Learning Worked Like a Game? Gamification is defined as the practice of applying game mechanics outside the realm of games. It can be used for employee training, allowing employees to practice expected skills and knowledge in a pressure-free environment.

Gallery 05 · Sci-Fi

Science Fiction

Science fiction creates strange worlds, but those worlds often expose familiar problems from our own reality.

A Different World. Familiar Problems. Science fiction can make us imagine planets, societies, and situations far removed from our own. But according to the module, science fiction is not simply about escaping reality. Its unusual settings can help readers examine social issues and problems that exist in the real world.

The Ones Who Walk Away From Omelas

Asks what people are willing to live with in order to have a crimeless society.

The Handmaid's Tale

Explores questions about women, their bodies, religion, and social structures.

FICTIONAL WORLD → What problems exist here?
🔗 Where do we see similar problems in reality? Social inequality, autonomy, justice, resistance, and the tension between individual rights and collective order.

📚 Featured Literary Work

Flicker Fade Gone by Carljoe Javier follows a young boy who finds comfort and belonging in an arcade. He becomes attached to video games because they allow him to escape reality and become someone different. As he grows older, he starts working at the arcade and develops feelings for a girl who often visits. Feeling invisible and disconnected from the real world, he becomes increasingly absorbed in the virtual world. In the end, he mysteriously disappears while playing a game, blurring the line between reality and fantasy.
